For a SaaS company with $2.16B in FCF, a D/E above 1.0 is unusual. If you dig into the balance sheet, Palantir’s high debt-like metrics often correlate with **Operating Leases** secured by long-term government contracts. However, the real red flag is the **Current Ratio of 7.22**. A current ratio that high in a software company isn't "efficiency"; it’s often a sign of **excessive uncollected receivables** or deep cash reserves that are not being returned to shareholders efficiently. Combined with the "low base" revenue jump, I smell aggressive revenue recognition or massive deferred revenue releases.
* **Earnings Quality Discrepancy:** The analyst claims FCF aligns with Net Income. Let’s do the math: $473B Market Cap at 76x P/S implies ~$6.16B in Revenue. If Net Margin is 49%, Net Income is ~$3B. It implies high capital expenditure, stock-based compensation (which dilutes shareholders and isn't cash), or aggressive working capital management that isn't converting to cash. That is a classic sign of "paper profits."

### 2. THESIS KILLERS

### 3. ASSUMPTION STRESS TEST

The counter-argument is that AIP is becoming commoditized. Every major cloud provider (AWS, Azure, GCP) and every data vendor (Databricks, Snowflake) is bundling these capabilities for free or at a nominal cost. The assumption that Palantir’s "ontology" is irreplaceable is failing when you can get 80% of the value for 20% of the price from a hyperscaler’s native AI service.
* **NRR (Net Revenue Expansion) Decay:** The analyst hand-waves "unprecedented surge in NRR." High NRR is normal in early SaaS, but as clients become more mature, expansion slows.
