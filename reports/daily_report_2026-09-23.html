### 1. Accounting Red Flags: The Leverage is Absurd
You listed **Debt/Equity at 6.33**.
Do you know what that means? For every $1 of equity, Micron has $6.33 of debt. In a cyclical semiconductor company, especially one entering a potential downturn or margin compression phase, this is a ticking time bomb.
* **The Trap:** Semis are capital intensive. If the AI cycle cools even slightly, EBITDA drops. With 6.3x leverage, debt service consumes free cash flow that ought to be going to dividends (if they had any) or buybacks.
* **The Reality Check:** While memory has a high gross margin currently (72.6%), that number does not survive contact with a price war. Look at 2022/2023. When DRAM/NAND prices crashed, margins went to zero or negative. With that debt load, they are exposed to refinancing risks if rates stay high or credit spreads widen. The 3.425 Current Ratio looks great on paper, but it’s likely bloated with days sales outstanding (DSO) from big customers who might delay payments if they sense a slowdown.

### 2. Thesis Killers: The "AI Infinite Horizon" Myth
**The Single Event:** A 10% drop in DRAM/NAND contract prices next quarter.
* Why this killer? The entire current valuation (stock at ~$1,000+ after splits) assumes *infinite* demand for HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) and standard DRAM from AI data centers.
* If even one major hyperscaler (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) pauses capex by 10% for one quarter, the supply/demand balance shifts. Memory chips are commodities. They do not have pricing power like NVDA. NVDA sells a product; MU sells a commodity that just happens to be in short supply right now.
* **News Anchor:** The news mentions "Micron Stock Is Back Above $1,000." This is an all-time high territory. That means the market has priced in *perfection*. Any deviation from a perfect AI capex cycle is catastrophic for the price.

### 3. Assumption Stress Test: What Must Be True?
For this bull case to play out, you must believe three things will happen simultaneously:
1. **AI Capex Never Slows:** Hyperscalers will continue to build data centers at record pace for another 3-5 years. *Realistic?* Historically, tech capex is cyclical. They are currently in a euphoric phase. It *will* mean-revert.
2. **No Supply Response:** SK Hynix and Samsung will *not* aggressively ramp up HBM capacity. *Realistic?* No. They have already announced massive capex increases to capture this AI wave. Once HBM supply catches up (12-18 months), margins will compress.
3. **Cyclicality is Dead:** The most dangerous assumption in semiconductor investing is "this time is different; the cycle is over." It is not. NAND is already seeing signs of oversupply. If DRAM follows, the earnings acceleration you noted ("EPS Accelerating: True") will reverse violently.

### 4. Valuation Risk: Pricing For Perfection
If the stock is near $1,000 (or post-split equivalents), the P/E is likely stretched beyond historical cycle peaks.
* **Multiple Risk:** Semiconductor stocks trade at 8-15x trailing earnings at the top of a cycle before the crash, and 40-60x at the bottom. Are you paying 30-40x forward earnings? If so, you need **sustained** growth. You cannot have **cyclical** growth.
* **The Kill Zone:** If the stock is up 100%+ in a year, it is pricing in several years of ideal outcomes. Any guidance cut of 5% could easily trigger a 20-30% drawdown.

### 5. Competitive Threats: The Memory Wars Return
* **SK Hynix:** They are ahead in HBM3E adoption. If they cut prices by 5-10% to protect share, MU’s 72.6% gross margin erodes rapidly.
* **Samsung:** They are desperate. After years of underperformance, they are pouring billions into HBM. In memory, the largest player often wins on scale and price. MU is a challenger, not the leader, in global DRAM market share.
* **Chinese Threats:** CXMT and YMTC are closing the gap in standard DRAM and NAND. If AI demand slows for *standard* memory (not just HBM), Chinese surpluses will flood the market, crushing prices.
